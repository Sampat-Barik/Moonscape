## 2. Spatial Resolution & Instrument Limitations

### 2.1 Altimetry Grid Resolution (5m vs. 20m vs. Optical Imagery)
Moonscape operates across two primary NASA Goddard Space Flight Center (GSFC) Planetary Geodynamics Data Archive (PGDA) datasets:
1. **NASA PGDA Product 78:** High-resolution LOLA/LROC merged DEM ($5.0\text{ m/pixel}$) available exclusively across selected candidate landing zones (e.g., Site 01 Shackleton Connecting Ridge, Site 04 Malapert Mountain).
2. **NASA PGDA Product 90:** Regional LOLA South Pole DEM ($20.0\text{ m/pixel}$) covering latitudes south of $-80.0^\circ$.

| Dataset | Grid Resolution | Vertical Accuracy | Slope Baseline ($3\times 3$) | Smallest Resolvable Feature |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **PGDA Product 78** | $5.0\text{ m/pixel}$ | $\pm 0.34\text{ m}$ | $15.0\text{ m}$ | $\sim 15 - 20\text{ m}$ crater rim / mound |
| **PGDA Product 90** | $20.0\text{ m/pixel}$ | $\pm 1.50\text{ m}$ | $60.0\text{ m}$ | $\sim 60 - 80\text{ m}$ topographic feature |
| **LROC NAC Targeted** | $0.5 - 2.0\text{ m/pixel}$ | Optical reflectance | Sub-meter stereo | $1.0 - 3.0\text{ m}$ individual boulder |

### 2.2 The Sub-Pixel Boulder Hazard Reality
- **Lander Undercarriage Risk:** Human and cargo lunar landers (such as Apollo LM, Artemis HLS, and CLPS commercial landers) have undercarriage engine clearance margins between $0.30\text{m}$ and $0.60\text{m}$.
- **Detection Boundary:** A $1.0\text{m}$ to $2.0\text{m}$ boulder cannot be resolved in a $5\text{m/pixel}$ grid and is entirely invisible in a $20\text{m/pixel}$ grid.
- **Moonscape Mitigation & Boundary:** Moonscape uses scale-dependent Root-Mean-Square (RMS) residual height deviation (Barker et al. 2016, 2021) across $15\text{m}$ and $60\text{m}$ baselines as a *statistical proxy* for surface roughness. However, a site with low RMS roughness may still harbor isolated meter-scale boulders. Targeted high-resolution LROC NAC optical stereo imagery is mandatory prior to landing commitment.

## 3. Topographic Slope Derivation & Scale-Dependence

Topographic slope is not an intrinsic point scalar; its value depends directly on the physical baseline length ($\Delta x$) across which elevation gradients are evaluated:
$$\text{Slope}(\Delta x) = \arctan\left(\sqrt{\left(\frac{\partial z}{\partial x}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{\partial z}{\partial y}\right)^2}\right)$$

1. **Slope Flattening at Coarse Baselines:** Slopes measured across a $60\text{m}$ baseline (LOLA 20m) systematically underestimate steep micro-topography compared to slopes measured across a $15\text{m}$ baseline (LOLA 5m). Local breaklines, crater lip overhangs, and small mounds are smoothed away.
2. **Landing Stability Baseline:** Dynamic lander tip-over depends on the differential elevation across the footpad span ($10\text{m} - 15\text{m}$). Therefore, only PGDA Product 78 ($5\text{m}$) slopes align with the true structural footprint of Artemis HLS class landers. Regional PGDA Product 90 slopes should be interpreted with a $+3.0^\circ$ to $+5.0^\circ$ safety margin.

## 4. Ephemeris & Solar/Earth Visibility Boundaries

### 4.1 Semi-Empirical Ephemeris vs. Full JPL SPICE
Moonscape implements high-performance analytical solar and Earth ephemeris algorithms based on Meeus (1998) and NASA JPL DE421 subsolar/sub-Earth coordinate series:
- **Accuracy:** Solar and Earth elevation angles are accurate to within $\pm 0.05^\circ$ across the 2024–2030 exploration epoch.
- **Limitation:** The current lightweight implementation does not dynamically integrate full JPL SPICE SPK/TLS/PCK kernels (`naif0012.tls`, `de440.bsp`, `moon_pa_de421.bpc`) to calculate higher-order relativistic time corrections or second-order lunar physical librations.

### 4.2 Distant Topographic Shadowing (Horizon Obstacle Horizon)
- At the lunar South Pole, the Sun never rises more than $1.54^\circ$ above the local geoid. Under such extreme grazing angles, a topographic feature located $30\text{km}$ to $50\text{km}$ away can cast shadows across the landing zone.
- Moonscape's local radial horizon ray-tracing calculates obstruction within the local elevation DEM window ($500\text{m}$ to $2000\text{m}$ radius). While regional highlands (e.g., Malapert Mountain) are modeled, micro-shadows cast by distant terrain features beyond the loaded raster extent are subject to unmodeled shadow ingress.

### 4.3 Lunar Libration & Earth RF Multipath Margin
- **Libration Dipping:** Due to the Moon's orbital eccentricity and obliquity, the apparent position of the Earth wobbles by up to $\pm 8.0^\circ$ in latitude and longitude over a 27.3-day nodical period.
- **RF Multipath Interference:** Near the horizon ($< 2.0^\circ$ elevation), ground reflections from surrounding crater rims produce phase-delayed multipath RF cancellation. Moonscape assumes a conservative $+2.0^\circ$ clearance margin above the terrain horizon for nominal direct-to-Earth communication.

## 5. Machine Learning Surrogate & Hazard Model Boundaries

### 5.1 Tabular ML Surrogate Scope
- The Random Forest suitability surrogate (`landing_suitability_rf_v1.joblib`) was trained on 3,000 synthetic and semi-empirical candidate zones across the South Pole.
- **Role:** It acts as a rapid ($< 3\text{ms}$) screening emulator of complex multi-criteria scoring and Monte Carlo uncertainty propagation.
- **Boundary:** The ML model does not replace physical engineering analysis. In any case where an engineering threshold is violated (e.g. slope $> 15.0^\circ$), the hard constraint gating engine strictly overrides the ML model prediction and enforces disqualification.

### 5.2 Computer Vision Hazard Model Apollo Bias
- Deep learning vision models trained on historical Apollo mission surface photography were captured under high solar elevations ($> 15.0^\circ$) near the lunar equator.
- **South Pole Optical Invalidation:** Applying these models to South Pole orbital imagery produces severe false positives and false negatives due to:
  1. Elongated grazing shadows extending kilometers across small obstacles.
  2. Permanently Shadowed Regions (PSRs) where optical reflectance drops to zero.
  3. Severe polar stereographic distortion at high latitudes.
- **Moonscape Policy:** Computer vision hazard predictions are presented with mandatory scientific warning banners, and topographic hazard detection relies primarily on LOLA laser altimetry derivatives rather than unvalidated optical classifiers.

## 6. Summary of Engineering Best Practices

When presenting Moonscape evaluations to mission stakeholders, always adhere to the following principles:
1. Always state that suitability scores represent **relative screening indicators**, not flight safety certifications.
2. Verify whether an analysis was performed on high-resolution ($5\text{m}$) or regional ($20\text{m}$) DEM data before drawing conclusions about landing gear clearance.
3. For landing zones within $5\text{km}$ of crater walls, inspect the time-series solar illumination curve to evaluate the impact of grazing shadow ingress.
